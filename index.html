A statistical mirror, not a prophecy

Survival Mirror

This isn't a death clock. It doesn't know your death date — nobody's software does. What it can show you is a survival curve: how a population that looks like you, on paper, tends to age. Every number below comes with its source, its uncertainty, and an honest list of everything it can't see.

Population data: national life tables for 236 countries — the U.S. Social Security Administration's own 2023 table for the United States, and the UN Population Division Data Portal's 2023 estimate (World Population Prospects, 2024 Revision) everywhere else. Pick your country below; the source and its reliability are shown with your result. Full citations, including how ages beyond 100 are modeled, are under “Full source citations” further down.

Before we start

Do you have a diagnosis your doctor has described as terminal or significantly life-limiting?

This model accounts for three specific diagnosis clusters further down (cardiometabolic history, kidney function, and COPD), but it has no way to account for a terminal or significantly life-limiting diagnosis, or for anything outside those three clusters. Answering honestly here matters more than anything else on this page.

Then this tool isn't going to give you anything useful, and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise. This model can weigh a handful of diagnosed chronic conditions, but it cannot meaningfully estimate a prognosis once a terminal or significantly life-limiting diagnosis is involved — that number lives with your care team, using information this page will never have.

If you'd like to see how the method works anyway — out of curiosity, or to understand what it's built from — you're welcome to keep reading below without entering your own information.

That's a fair thing to keep to yourself. Worth knowing before you go on: if a terminal or significantly life-limiting diagnosis applies to you, everything this page produces will be optimistic by an amount it has no way to estimate. The ten questions further down can't detect it.

    What this can't see

    The model's blind spots

      Nothing on this page is sent anywhere. There's no server to send it to, and the page's own Content-Security-Policy sets connect-src 'none', which blocks scripted network requests outright rather than just promising not to make them. Open DevTools → Network and reload; you'll see one HTML file and nothing else. It works with your wifi off. Two honest caveats: a meta-tag CSP governs scripted requests, not a link you click yourself, and your answers are written to this browser's localStorage so they survive a reload — “Wipe everything” deletes them. Bulk mode's spreadsheet upload works the same way: it's read locally through the browser's File API and never leaves your device, and the Network tab stays just as empty. Bulk mode's spreadsheet reading and writing runs on the SheetJS Community Edition library (Apache License 2.0, version 0.20.3), loaded from xlsx.full.min.js sitting next to this file rather than a CDN, so it still loads with no network request — see sheetjs.com. Keep that file alongside this one; without it, bulk mode's spreadsheet reading and writing won't work. Nothing you upload in bulk mode is written to localStorage; it lives only in this tab and is gone on reload.
