A statistical mirror, not a prophecy

Survival Mirror

This isn't a death clock. It doesn't know your death date — nobody's software does. What it can show you is a survival curve: how a population that looks like you, on paper, tends to age. Every number below comes with its source, its uncertainty, and an honest list of everything it can't see.

Population data: U.S. Social Security Administration, 2023 period life table. If that isn't roughly your population, treat every number below as further off than it already is.

Before we start

Do you have a diagnosis your doctor has described as terminal or significantly life-limiting?

This model is built entirely from general-population lifestyle data. It has no way to account for a specific diagnosis, and answering honestly here matters more than anything else on this page.

Then this tool isn't going to give you anything useful, and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise. A lifestyle-hazard model built from healthy-population cohort data cannot meaningfully estimate a prognosis once a specific diagnosis is involved — that number lives with your care team, using information this page will never have.

If you'd like to see how the method works anyway — out of curiosity, or to understand what it's built from — you're welcome to keep reading below without entering your own information.

That's a fair thing to keep to yourself. Worth knowing before you go on: if a significant diagnosis does apply to you, everything this page produces will be optimistic by an amount it has no way to estimate. The seven questions can't detect it.

What this can't see

    Nothing on this page is sent anywhere. There's no server to send it to, and the page's own Content-Security-Policy sets connect-src 'none', which blocks scripted network requests outright rather than just promising not to make them. Open DevTools → Network and reload; you'll see one HTML file and nothing else. It works with your wifi off. Two honest caveats: a meta-tag CSP governs scripted requests, not a link you click yourself, and your answers are written to this browser's localStorage so they survive a reload — “Wipe everything” deletes them.
